PART I — THE BRAND’S CREATIVE FOUNDATION

1. WHAT WE ARE REALLY SELLING

We are not selling four bottles.

We are selling the feeling of having skincare figured out: knowing what to use, enjoying how it feels and feeling confident that the routine makes sense for your skin and your day.

Our products address specific skincare needs:

* Cleanse: Remove excess oil and daily buildup while maintaining hydration.
* Treat: Help control oil, even skin tone and hydrate.
* Moisturize: Support the skin barrier and provide lightweight, oil-conscious care.
* Protect: Deliver SPF 50+ protection with an invisible, lightweight, fast-absorbing finish.

The brand’s climate-adapted positioning makes these benefits more relevant to everyday life in Egypt.

2. OUR CUSTOMER

Primary audience: Women in Egypt, particularly urban Gen Z and Millennial consumers, with potential to resonate across the wider region.

She may be a student, a professional, a mother, a creative or someone whose day moves between several environments.

We must not reduce her to one stereotype.

What matters is that she wants skincare that:

* Addresses concerns she actually notices.
* Feels comfortable in hot or humid conditions.
* Doesn’t make an already busy day more complicated.
* Has ingredients and benefits she can understand.
* Is worth paying for.
* Doesn’t make exaggerated promises.

We speak to her intelligence, not her insecurities.

Core customer tension:

“I want to take care of my skin, but I don’t want another complicated routine or products that feel uncomfortable on my face.”

Brand answer:

“Four purposeful steps, made for the skin you have and the day you’re living.”

This is an internal strategic proposition, not a claim that everyone needs all four products.

3. OUR COPYWRITING SYSTEM

Every commercial section should use the following sequence when appropriate:

Recognize → Desire → Explain → Reassure → Act

Recognize: A familiar concern or everyday experience.

Desire: A clear, realistic result the customer wants.

Explain: The product benefit and relevant ingredients.

Reassure: Texture, suitability, routine order, delivery or other decision-making information.

Act: One clear next step.

Not every section needs all five elements. The homepage introduces the story; product pages do the detailed selling.

Example: moisturizer

Weak, ingredient-led:

“A ceramide moisturizer with niacinamide and Zinc PCA.”

Weak, generic benefit-led:

“Hydrates and strengthens the barrier.”

Our direction:

“Your skin needs moisture. Not the extra weight.”

“Even oily skin needs hydration. Daily Barrier helps strengthen your skin barrier and control excess oil, with a lightweight feel that makes moisturizing easy to keep in your routine.”

The headline sells the desired experience. The body establishes the benefits. The ingredients provide supporting explanation further down.

4. BRAND PERSONALITY

Observant

We notice the details of her everyday life without pretending to know everything about her.

Confident

We make clear statements instead of hiding behind vague language.

Intelligent

We explain skincare simply without talking down to customers.

Warm

We sound human, not clinical or corporate.

Contemporary

We feel current without depending on trends or slang.

Considered

We use fewer, better words. We do not manufacture urgency or insecurity.

We sound like this

“Your skin needs moisture. Not the extra weight.”

“Sun protection without the sunscreen feeling.”

“Less shine. More even-looking skin.”

“Your routine should fit your day. Not the other way around.”

We do not sound like this

“Unlock your ultimate radiant glow.”

“Revolutionary next-generation skin transformation.”

“Say goodbye to all your imperfections.”

“Clinically flawless skin in seven days.”

“Your skin deserves the very best.”

5. THE EGYPTIAN ARABIC VOICE

The Arabic is written for Egyptian women, not translated mechanically from English.

Register: Natural, polished Egyptian Arabic.

Address: Feminine singular in marketing and shopping interactions.

Personality: Warm, modern, clear and confident.

Vocabulary: Everyday Egyptian expressions, with familiar skincare terminology where it improves clarity.

Avoid: Excessive fusha, awkward literal translations, exaggerated slang, gender-neutral constructions that make the writing feel impersonal, and unnecessarily complicated ingredient terminology.

English-to-Arabic creative examples

English: “Your skin needs moisture. Not the extra weight.”

Arabic: “بشرتك محتاجة ترطيب، مش إحساس تقيل.”

English: “Sun protection without the sunscreen feeling.”

Arabic: “حماية من الشمس من غير الإحساس التقيل.”

English: “Less shine. More even-looking skin.”

Arabic: “لمعان أقل. ولون بشرة أهدى وأكثر تجانسًا.”

English: “Your routine should fit your day. Not the other way around.”

Arabic: “روتينك المفروض يناسب يومك، مش العكس.”

The Arabic communicates the benefit and emotion, even when the sentence construction changes.

PART IX — FINAL EDITORIAL RULES

1. Every major selling headline must communicate a customer benefit, a recognizable tension or a distinctive brand idea.
2. The homepage must not repeat the same climate list in every section.
3. The official four steps must always be Cleanse, Treat, Moisturize and Protect.
4. English and Arabic must communicate equivalent benefits and emotions, not necessarily identical sentence structures.
5. Egyptian Arabic must address women naturally, without excessive slang or formal translation.
6. Packaging claims are the factual foundation. Customer-friendly expressions must remain faithful to them.
7. Pollution stays in the brand’s environmental context. Specific anti-pollution efficacy is not claimed without evidence.
8. No invented clinical outcomes, percentages, testimonials, time-to-result claims or founder stories.
9. Product pages must explain why the product matters before explaining every ingredient.
10. Sets must communicate why a customer should choose them, what is included and the real saving.
11. Every page should have one primary conversion objective.
12. Keep transactional copy clear and practical. Cleverness must never make checkout or customer support harder.
13. Review the copy inside the actual designs, particularly on mobile.
14. Do not change approved brand direction during factual or technical implementation.
15. The English and Arabic versions must receive equal editorial and design attention.
